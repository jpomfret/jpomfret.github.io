# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: DBAs, data engineers, and PowerShell users looking for practical, runnable automation content — SQL Server, Azure SQL, dbatools, and DevOps topics.

Secondary: people encountering Jess through speaking (Sessionize), the co-authored book, or the "Finding Data Friends" podcast, who are evaluating her as a speaker/author/community figure rather than searching for a specific technical fix.

## Product Purpose

A personal technical blog and professional hub for Jess Pomfret. Publishes tutorials and how-tos on SQL Server, Azure SQL, dbatools, PowerShell automation, and DevOps, and surfaces her book, podcast, and speaking to build community presence and professional credibility.

## Positioning

Practical, tested automation content grounded in real dbatools maintainership and production experience — broader than a single-tool blog, spanning SQL Server, Azure, and DevOps — combined with a personal voice and visible community involvement (podcast, speaking, co-authored book).

## Operating Context

Static site built with Hugo (`hugo-theme-stack` v3 theme as a Go module), content authored in Markdown under `blog/content`. Deployed to jesspomfret.com (see `CNAME`) via GitHub Pages. Posts include code samples (PowerShell/T-SQL), screenshots, and cover images. Blog images are produced from a Canva template (see Brand Commitments).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Hugo static site generator; content archive runs 2018–present under `blog/content/post`, plus standalone pages (Buy the Book, Finding Data Friends, archives, search).
- Social links: GitHub, Bluesky, Instagram, Sessionize, LinkedIn.
- Disqus comments enabled.
- Current license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, inherited from the theme starter, not deliberately chosen — see Brand Commitments for the pending change.

## Product Principles

1. Practical over theoretical — technical posts should be runnable and tested, not just conceptual.
2. Breadth within data: SQL Server, Azure SQL, dbatools, PowerShell, and DevOps, not one narrow tool.
3. Personal voice and community visibility (book, podcast, speaking) are part of the product, not separate from it.
4. A visual overhaul must preserve the existing content archive and its URLs/structure.
5. The octopus/purple-blue Brand Kit is the committed future direction; the legacy theme-default look is being retired, not preserved as a fallback.
